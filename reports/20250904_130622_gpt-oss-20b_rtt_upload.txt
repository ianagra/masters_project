## QoS Assessment Report: rtt_upload Approach  

### Executive Summary  
The analysis of 16 Raspberry Pi probes running NDT every 30 minutes reveals a clear dichotomy between two clusters of network behavior. Cluster 0 (high‑risk) is marked by short, event‑laden intervals, low throughput, and high RTTs, while Cluster 1 (low‑risk) enjoys longer, stable intervals with high throughput and low latency. The most critical clients—**client09, client12, client02**—and the single critical server—**rnp_rj**—are consistently associated with Cluster 0. The logistic regression highlights **rtt_download_std**, **throughput_download_mean**, **throughput_upload_std**, and **rtt_download_mean** as the strongest drivers of instability, with download latency variability and mean latency exerting the largest negative influence on cluster membership.

---

### Detailed Findings  

#### Cluster Characteristics  
- **Cluster 0 (High‑Risk)**  
  - Mean interval duration: 10.5 min  
  - Event frequency: 0.645 (≈ 64 %)  
  - Survival probability: 0.27 at 30 days  
  - Throughput: ~307 Mbps (download), ~281 Mbps (upload)  
  - RTT: ~96 ms (download), ~98 ms (upload)  
  - High variability in all metrics  

- **Cluster 1 (Low‑Risk)**  
  - Mean interval duration: 31.5 min  
  - Event frequency: 0.030 (≈ 3 %)  
  - Survival probability: 0.93 at 60 days  
  - Throughput: ~661 Mbps (download), ~660 Mbps (upload)  
  - RTT: ~18 ms (download), ~20 ms (upload)  
  - Low variability  

#### Most Critical Clients  
- **client09** – consistently unstable when contacting **gru03**; stable with other servers.  
- **client12** – long period of stability interrupted by high‑risk intervals with **gru03, gru05, gru06, rnp_sp**.  
- **client02** – intermittent instability tied to **rnp_rj** and **rnp_sp**.  

#### Most Critical Server  
- **rnp_rj** – the only server that appears in high‑risk intervals for **client01** and **client02**; shows elevated RTT mean and variance during those periods.  

#### Metrics with Greatest Impact on Stability  
- **rtt_download_std** – negative coefficient (–1.99). Larger variability in download latency dramatically increases the odds of being in the high‑risk cluster.  
- **rtt_download_mean** – negative coefficient (–0.66). Higher average download latency pushes intervals toward instability.  
- **throughput_download_mean** – positive coefficient (+1.32). Higher average download throughput favors the low‑risk cluster; lower throughput drives instability.  
- **throughput_upload_std** – positive coefficient (+0.83). Although upload variability is less critical than download latency, higher upload variability still correlates with the low‑risk cluster, suggesting that upload stability is a secondary indicator.  

---

### Recommended Actions  

- **Path‑specific diagnostics**  
  - Run `traceroute` and `mtr` from the affected clients (client09, client12, client02) to the problematic servers (gru03, gru05, gru06, rnp_sp, rnp_rj) during identified high‑risk intervals.  
  - Compare hop‑by‑hop RTTs and packet loss against baseline measurements to isolate the segment causing latency spikes.  

- **Routing and peering review**  
  - Inspect BGP routing tables and peering logs for the paths to **gru03** and **rnp_rj** during the high‑risk windows.  
  - Verify that no routing flaps or sub‑optimal path selections are occurring.  

- **QoS policy adjustment**  
  - Consider enforcing stricter QoS on the upstream links to the identified servers, prioritizing critical traffic and limiting bursty uploads that may contribute to upload variability.  

- **Continuous monitoring**  
  - Deploy real‑time dashboards that flag intervals where `rtt_download_std` or `rtt_download_mean` exceed predefined thresholds.  
  - Automate alerts for sudden drops in `throughput_download_mean` to pre‑empt stability degradation.  

- **Infrastructure review**  
  - Evaluate the capacity and health of the edge routers handling traffic to **gru03** and **rnp_rj**; upgrade if necessary to reduce congestion.  

Implementing these steps will pinpoint the root causes of the observed instability, enable targeted remediation, and improve overall QoS for the affected clients and servers.
