## QoS Assessment Report: rtt_upload Approach

### 1) Executive Summary
The rtt_upload-based analysis separates intervals into two distinct operational states. Cluster 0 exhibits a high-risk, unstable profile with short-lived intervals, frequent change events, higher RTTs, and lower/more variable throughput. Cluster 1 is low-risk and stable, with sustained low RTT and high throughput. Logistic regression identifies client09, client12, client02, client07, and server rnp_rj as most associated with the high-risk Cluster 0. Diagnoses show a recurrent, path-specific impairment toward site gru03 affecting multiple clients (client09, client12, client07), a client/server-path instability between client01/client02 and rnp_rj during May–June, and a late-window onset of instability to rnp_sp for client07.

### 2) Detailed Findings

- Cluster risk profiles (rtt_upload approach)
  - Cluster 0 (high risk): Short intervals (mean ~10.55, median ~4.39), high event_frequency (0.645), survival decay from 0.91 (day 1) to 0.11 (day 60/90). Higher RTTs (rtt_d ~96.35 ms; rtt_u ~98.06 ms) and lower, more variable throughput (down ~306.6 Mbps; up ~281.0 Mbps).
  - Cluster 1 (low risk): Long intervals (mean ~31.45, median ~28.98), very low event_frequency (0.030), strong survival (≥0.933 through day 60/90). Low RTTs (rtt_d ~17.95 ms; rtt_u ~20.41 ms) and higher, more stable throughput (down ~660.7 Mbps; up ~660.0 Mbps).

- Critical elements (logistic regression association to Cluster 0; negative coefficients decrease odds of Cluster 1)
  - client09 (coef −0.9923): strongest client-side tendency toward Cluster 0.
  - rnp_rj (coef −0.9031): strongest server-side association to Cluster 0.
  - client12 (coef −0.7821), client02 (coef −0.6515), client07 (coef −0.6036): additional strong associations to Cluster 0.

- Interval diagnoses for critical elements
  - client09:
    - Stable/low-risk (Cluster 1) to gru05, gru06, rnp_rj, rnp_sp with excellent throughput (down 831–905 Mbps; up 859–871 Mbps) and low RTT (12–20 ms).
    - High-risk exclusively to gru03: most of May–June at ~170/182 Mbps with RTT ~116–121 ms; 2024-06-28→07-01 severe dip (down 93.9 Mbps; up 100.4 Mbps; RTT ~212 ms); 2024-07-09→07-19 RTT normalizes (~15 ms) and upload recovers (~867 Mbps) but download remains ~172 Mbps; 2024-07-19→07-23 download unstable (mean ~147 Mbps; very high std) despite low RTT and high upload. Cluster 0 intervals heavily event-flagged.
    - Interpretation: path/server-specific issue toward gru03; post-07-09 routing/latency improved, but downstream (server-to-client) throughput remains unstable.

  - rnp_rj (server):
    - 44 intervals: 33 Cluster 1 (~75%), 11 Cluster 0 (~25%). All Cluster 0 are event-flagged.
    - client01: Stable low RTT baseline outside two degradation windows:
      - 2024-05-07→06-01: rtt_d ~18.5–21.5 ms (up from ~7 ms), variable rtt_u; indication of elevated latency.
      - 2024-07-11→07-18: fluctuating RTT and download mean drop to ~495 Mbps (std ~72 Mbps).
    - client02: 2024-05-08→06-15 prolonged Cluster 0 with uplink RTT elevation/variance (rtt_u up to ~25–26 ms) and download dips/variance (down ~554 Mbps, std ~78 Mbps), then recovery.
    - Other clients (03–16): entirely Cluster 1 with rnp_rj.
    - Interpretation: degradations are path-specific to client01/client02 toward rnp_rj; no server-wide instability detected.

  - client12:
    - Spent 9/17 intervals in Cluster 1 and 8/17 in Cluster 0.
    - gru03: 2024-05-06→07-09 persistent high-risk with severe RTT inflation (up to ~223–229 ms) and throughput collapse (down ~81 Mbps during 06-28→07-01). 07-01→07-09 remains unstable (RTT ~75–105 ms). After 2024-07-09, flips to Cluster 1 with strong throughput (~670–748 Mbps) and low RTT (~14–19 ms).
    - gru05: Mostly stable, brief Cluster 0 2024-07-08→07-14 driven by higher/variable download RTT (~26.7 ms); throughput largely unchanged.
    - rnp_sp: Short Cluster 0 2024-07-07→07-17 with elevated upload RTT (~14.33 ms vs ~11.68 prior), then recovery.
    - rnp_rj and gru06: Stable Cluster 1 throughout.
    - Interpretation: long-standing path issue to gru03 resolved around 2024-07-09; minor transient RTT increases to gru05/rnp_sp in mid-July.

  - client02:
    - Mostly stable (Cluster 1 = 7/12) across gru03, gru05, gru06; typical metrics down ~582–593 Mbps, up ~296–301 Mbps; RTT down ~9–12 ms (SP/GRU), ~16–17 ms (RJ).
    - High-risk only with RNP paths:
      - rnp_sp: 2024-05-06→05-08 modest RTT increase (rtt_d ~11.07 ms) and higher variability; then stable.
      - rnp_rj: 2024-05-08→06-15 multiple Cluster 0 intervals; worst 2024-06-02→06-04 with down ~554 Mbps (std ~78 Mbps) and rtt_u ~25.4 ms with high variance; lower download persisted until 06-15; recovered thereafter with rtt_u still moderately higher than early May.
    - Interpretation: RJ path/server-specific instability in May–June; no issues to GRU sites.

  - client07:
    - Mixed stability: 60% Cluster 1, 40% Cluster 0.
    - gru03: All intervals in Cluster 0. 2024-06-28→07-01 severe (down/up ~95/104 Mbps; RTT ~207/212 ms). 07-09→07-23 RTT improves (~20 ms) and throughput means rise (~538/619 Mbps) but with very high variability; still classified as Cluster 0.
    - rnp_sp: Stable until 2024-07-16, then Cluster 0 (07-16→07-23) with highly variable downstream throughput (mean ~522 Mbps, std ~421 Mbps) while RTT stays low.
    - gru05, gru06, rnp_rj: Stable Cluster 1, high throughput (~760–860 Mbps down; ~550–790 Mbps up) and low RTT (~12–20 ms).
    - Interpretation: persistent path impairment to gru03 with partial recovery but ongoing instability; new throughput instability to rnp_sp starting mid-July with low RTT.

- Cross-cutting observations
  - Recurrent impairment to gru03 across client09, client12, and client07 with severe RTT inflation and throughput collapse around 2024-06-28→07-01, followed by post-2024-07-09 RTT normalization but lingering downstream throughput instability in some cases.
  - Path-specific degradations to rnp_rj confined to client01 and client02 during May–June; other clients remained stable, indicating no server-wide issue.
  - A late-window, throughput-only instability to rnp_sp emerges for client07 (07-16→07-23) with RTT remaining low.

### 3) Recommended Actions

- Path validation and routing analysis
  - Run traceroute/MTR in both directions for:
    - client09/client12/client07 ↔ gru03 (peak and off-peak), focusing on pre- vs post-2024-07-09 paths.
    - client01/client02 ↔ rnp_rj during comparable dayparts to the May–June degradation windows.
    - client07 ↔ rnp_sp, comparing pre- (≤07-15) and post-onset (≥07-16) paths.
  - Review BGP/IGP change logs and route policies affecting GRU03, RNP-RJ, and RNP-SP around:
    - 2024-06-28→07-01 and 2024-07-09 (gru03),
    - 2024-05-08→06-15 (rnp_rj),
    - 2024-07-16 (rnp_sp).

- Server and interface telemetry
  - Check gru03 server and adjacent interfaces: CPU/NIC utilization, queue occupancy, drops, AQM behavior, TCP retransmissions, especially 06-28→07-01 and immediately after 07-09.
  - Inspect rnp_rj ingress/egress facing client01/client02 networks for errors/drops/queueing during 05-08→06-15; corroborate with traffic/load on the server.
  - Audit rnp_sp for throughput-plane anomalies starting 07-16 (NIC/queue saturation, shaping changes).

- Directional performance testing
  - Run parallel iperf3/NDT from affected clients to:
    - gru03 vs a healthy control (e.g., gru06) to isolate download-only degradation.
    - rnp_rj and rnp_sp vs controls, during peak and off-peak.
  - Augment with active loss/latency probes (e.g., OWAMP/TWAMP) to detect microbursts or intermittent loss where RTT remains low but throughput is unstable.

- Configuration and policy review
  - Verify any mid-July adjustments that could explain RTT normalization to gru03 but residual downstream instability (QoS/shaping, asymmetric routing, ECN/AQM configs).
  - Compare queue/traffic shaping policies applied to client01/client02 paths versus other clients that remained stable to rnp_rj.

- Monitoring and escalation
  - Set targeted alerts for:
    - RTT_u spikes >2× baseline and download throughput variance surges to gru03 and rnp_rj.
    - Sudden increases in downstream throughput variance to rnp_sp.
  - If path asymmetry or upstream congestion is confirmed, engage peering partners and RNP operations with the time-bound evidence above.
