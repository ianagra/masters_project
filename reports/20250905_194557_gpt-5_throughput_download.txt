## QoS Assessment Report: throughput_download Approach

## Executive Summary
Across 16 probes testing RNP targets, clustering separates intervals into a high‑risk state (Cluster 0) with short, volatile periods and degraded KPI profiles, and a low‑risk state (Cluster 1) with long, stable operation and superior performance. Despite this being the throughput_download approach, the logistic model shows RTT features—especially rtt_download_mean and rtt_download_std—are the dominant discriminants of instability. Client02 and Client07 are strongly associated with the high‑risk cluster, with persistent or episodic degradations focused on RNP‑RJ and RNP‑SP. Server rnp_rj is the most critical server by coefficient, exhibiting partner‑specific long high‑risk epochs, notably with client02 and client07. Evidence points to path‑specific congestion/queuing and, in some cases, severe download‑throughput variance under modest RTT, suggesting intermittent loss or queue drops.

## Detailed Findings

- Cluster profiles (risk posture)
  - Cluster 0 (High‑risk, unstable):
    - Persistence: short mean duration 10.64 days (median 6.65); event_frequency 0.738; survival decays rapidly to 0.18 by day 15 onward.
    - Throughput: lower means (download 452, upload 429) with high variability (std ≈265–285).
    - Latency: higher RTT means (down 59.6, up 60.6) with very large variability (std ≈63–66).
  - Cluster 1 (Low‑risk, stable):
    - Persistence: long mean duration 24.29 days (median 19.62); event_frequency 0.078; strong survival to 0.792 through days 60–90.
    - Throughput: higher means (download 632.5, upload 684.3).
    - Latency: lower RTT means (down 16.0, up 18.9) and lower variability (down std 4.38; up std 19.51).

- Most critical clients (by logistic regression, coefficients are log‑odds toward Cluster 1; negative implies higher risk/Cluster 0)
  - client02: −1.952 (strong association with Cluster 0).
  - client07: −1.734 (strong association with Cluster 0).
  - client13: −0.606 (moderate association with Cluster 0).

- Most critical servers
  - rnp_rj: −1.368 (strong association with Cluster 0).

- Metrics with greatest impact on stability (model coefficients for log‑odds of Cluster 1)
  - rtt_download_mean: −1.454 (strongest driver of instability). Higher download RTT sharply increases odds of Cluster 0.
  - rtt_download_std: −0.994 (strong driver). Higher download RTT jitter increases odds of Cluster 0.
  - rtt_upload_mean: −0.624 (secondary driver). Higher upload RTT increases odds of Cluster 0.
  - rtt_upload_std: +0.897 (tilts to stability). Higher upload RTT variability is associated with Cluster 1 in this dataset.

- Critical client diagnoses
  - client02
    - Intervals: 24/28 (86%) in Cluster 0 overall.
    - Target specificity: rnp_rj 15/15 and rnp_sp 9/9 in Cluster 0; gru03/05/06 all Cluster 1.
    - Metrics: Elevated download and upload RTT toward rnp_rj (down ≈16–17 ms vs ≈11–12 ms to stable targets; up ≈17.5–25 ms), aligning with RTT‑driven instability. For rnp_sp, Cluster 0 occurs despite low RTT means/variability, suggesting non‑RTT features (e.g., throughput volatility) contributed.
  - client07
    - Intervals: 17/27 (63%) in Cluster 0.
    - Time/targets: From mid‑June, rnp_sp and rnp_rj shift to Cluster 0; gru03 remains Cluster 0 until mid‑July, then recovers; gru05/06 stable.
    - Metrics: Clear RTT inflation/jitter to gru03 during Cluster 0 (down mean ~120–207 ms). rnp_sp’s transition coincides with increased rtt_download_std (~0.8 → ~6.1 ms). rnp_rj shows severe download throughput volatility and drops (std up to ~405; mean ~377–380 Mbps) under relatively stable RTT, indicating intermittent loss/queuing.
  - client13
    - Intervals: 9/19 (47%) in Cluster 0.
    - Time/targets: Prolonged Cluster 0 toward gru03 until 2024‑07‑17 (worst window down RTT mean ≈197 ms, std ≈46 ms), then recovery; brief Cluster 0 to rnp_rj (early–mid July) and rnp_sp (2024‑07‑17→07‑19); gru05/06 always stable.
    - Metrics: High download RTT/jitter on gru03 dominates risk; some episodes align with event=1.

- Critical server diagnosis (rnp_rj)
  - Cluster balance: 42/77 (54.6%) Cluster 1 vs 35/77 (45.4%) Cluster 0.
  - Concentrated high‑risk interactions:
    - client02: 16/16 Cluster 0 for entire window (83.5 days).
    - client07: 7/8 Cluster 0 from 2024‑06‑16 onward (throughput collapses).
    - Additional shorter high‑risk episodes with client01, client04, client13, client16.
  - Metrics: High rtt_download_mean and rtt_download_std are common during Cluster 0 (e.g., client01 mid‑May spike). Some high‑risk windows show throughput collapses with only modest RTT elevation, suggesting intermittent packet loss/queue drops.

## Recommended Actions

- Path and hop‑level localization
  - Run parallel MTR/Paris‑traceroute (both directions) from client02 and client07 to rnp_rj and rnp_sp, plus to a stable control (e.g., gru06), during peak and off‑peak for 48 hours to identify hops with elevated RTT/jitter or loss.
  - Repeat targeted tests from rnp_rj toward the affected client prefixes to detect asymmetry.

- Throughput variance and loss validation
  - For client07→rnp_rj, execute high‑frequency NDT/iPerf download tests to confirm variance under steady RTT; correlate with packet loss/retransmission counters and ECN marks on the path.

- Capacity and queueing audit
  - Inspect interface utilization, buffering, and drop counters on interconnects/peerings toward RNP‑RJ and RNP‑SP over 2024‑06‑12 onward; focus on mid‑June onset of Cluster 0 for client07 and persistent exposure for client02.
  - Verify QoS/queue policies for downstream paths to the affected access regions.

- Routing change analysis
  - Compare BGP paths and IGP/ECMP decisions before/after mid‑June for destinations behind rnp_rj and rnp_sp; roll back or adjust if a suboptimal path correlates with the degradations.

- Server‑side correlation
  - Coordinate with RNP to review server load and NIC/stack telemetry during high‑risk windows, especially for rnp_rj in mid‑June to late July.

- Controlled A/B verification
  - During suspected problem windows, run simultaneous tests toward rnp_rj/rnp_sp and stable targets (gru05/06) from the same probes to confirm path‑specific effects and to rule out client‑side bottlenecks.
