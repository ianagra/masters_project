## QoS Assessment Report: throughput_download Approach  

### Executive Summary  
The throughput‑download analysis reveals a clear dichotomy between two stability regimes. Cluster 0, representing the high‑risk segment, exhibits markedly lower download/upload throughput (≈ 450 Mbps) and elevated RTTs (≈ 60 ms). Cluster 1, the low‑risk segment, enjoys healthy bandwidth (≈ 630 Mbps) and low latency (≈ 16 ms). The most influential predictors of instability are the download RTT mean and its standard deviation, followed by the upload RTT mean. Three clients—**client02**, **client07**, and **client13**—are disproportionately associated with Cluster 0, while the server **rnp_rj** is the primary high‑risk endpoint. Immediate remediation should target routing and peering paths that inflate download latency to these clients and server.

---

### Detailed Findings  

#### 1. Cluster Characteristics  
- **Cluster 0 (High‑Risk)**  
  - Mean interval duration: 10.6 days (median 6.7 days).  
  - Throughput: 452 Mbps (download) / 429 Mbps (upload).  
  - RTT: 59.6 ms (download) / 60.6 ms (upload).  
  - Event frequency: 0.738 (highly volatile).  

- **Cluster 1 (Low‑Risk)**  
  - Mean interval duration: 24.3 days (median 19.6 days).  
  - Throughput: 632 Mbps (download) / 684 Mbps (upload).  
  - RTT: 16.0 ms (download) / 18.9 ms (upload).  
  - Event frequency: 0.078 (stable).  

#### 2. Most Critical Clients  
- **client02** – coefficient **‑1.95**  
- **client07** – coefficient **‑1.73**  
- **client13** – coefficient **‑0.61**  

These clients spend the majority of their measurement windows in Cluster 0, indicating persistent instability.

#### 3. Most Critical Server  
- **rnp_rj** – coefficient **‑1.37**  
  - The server is the sole endpoint consistently classified as high‑risk across multiple clients.

#### 4. Metrics with Greatest Impact on Stability  
1. **rtt_download_mean** – coefficient **‑1.454**  
   - Higher average download latency dramatically increases the odds of being in Cluster 0.  
2. **rtt_download_std** – coefficient **‑0.994**  
   - Greater jitter in download latency pushes intervals toward the high‑risk cluster.  
3. **rtt_upload_mean** – coefficient **‑0.624**  
   - Elevated upload latency also contributes to instability, though to a lesser extent.  
4. **rtt_upload_std** – coefficient **+0.897**  
   - Surprisingly, higher upload jitter is associated with stability, indicating it is not a primary driver of instability.  

Throughput metrics, while differing between clusters, did not appear as strong predictors in the logistic regression and are therefore secondary to RTT characteristics.

---

### Recommended Actions  

- **Routing Analysis**  
  - Run `traceroute` and `mtr` from the client locations (client02, client07, client13) to **rnp_rj** during high‑risk intervals.  
  - Compare hop‑by‑hop latency and packet loss with baseline traces to **gru** sites (e.g., gru05) that exhibit stable performance.  
  - Identify any consistently high‑latency or lossy hops and coordinate with upstream ISPs or BGP peers to adjust routing policies.

- **Peering & BGP Policy Review**  
  - Examine peering agreements and BGP attributes affecting paths to **rnp_rj**.  
  - Consider implementing route filtering or traffic engineering to favor lower‑latency paths for the affected clients.

- **Server‑Side Diagnostics**  
  - From **rnp_rj**, perform reverse traceroutes to the high‑risk clients during Cluster 0 periods.  
  - Verify that server‑side routing and upstream links are not the bottleneck; if they are, engage with the server’s ISP to optimize downstream paths.

- **Continuous Monitoring**  
  - Deploy real‑time alerts for download RTT mean > 50 ms and download RTT std > 5 ms on the affected client‑server pairs.  
  - Log these events to correlate with any subsequent routing changes or network incidents.

- **Throughput Enhancement (Secondary)**  
  - While RTT is the primary issue, ensure that link capacities are not saturated by monitoring throughput trends.  
  - If throughput drops below 500 Mbps for extended periods, investigate potential congestion or QoS policy conflicts.

Implementing these steps will directly address the latency‑driven instability identified in the throughput‑download approach, improving overall QoS for the most affected clients and the **rnp_rj** server.
