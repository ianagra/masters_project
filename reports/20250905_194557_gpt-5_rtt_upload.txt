## QoS Assessment Report: rtt_upload Approach

## 1) Executive Summary
Two distinct operational profiles emerged. Cluster 1 is stable, long‑lived, and high‑performing; Cluster 0 is unstable, short‑lived, and marked by degraded throughput and elevated latency. The most influential discriminator of instability is downstream RTT jitter (rtt_download_std), followed by lower download throughput. The clients most associated with high‑risk behavior are client09, client12, and client02. Server rnp_rj shows client‑specific degradation (not systemic), primarily with client01 and client02. The most persistent issue is a path/server‑specific problem toward “gru03” affecting client09 and client12 until 2024‑07‑09, with client09 retaining a download throughput cap to “gru03” even after RTT normalizes.

## 2) Detailed Findings

- Cluster risk profiles
  - Cluster 1 (stable): Long intervals (~31.45 days), very low event frequency (3%), high throughput (DL/UL ≈ 661/660 Mb/s), low RTT (DL/UL ≈ 18/20 ms), high survival.
  - Cluster 0 (high‑risk): Short intervals (~10.55 days), high event frequency (64.5%), lower throughput (DL/UL ≈ 307/281 Mb/s), high RTT (DL/UL ≈ 96/98 ms), rapid survival decay.

- Most critical clients (highest association with high‑risk Cluster 0 by logistic coefficients)
  - client09 (coef −0.992): Strongest high‑risk association.
    - Diagnosis: All high‑risk intervals confined to “gru03”. From 2024‑05‑03 to 2024‑07‑09, elevated RTT and jitter with low download throughput. After 2024‑07‑09, RTT normalizes (~13–16 ms) but download throughput to “gru03” remains capped (~147–172 Mb/s). All other targets stable and high‑performing.
  - client12 (coef −0.782): High‑risk association.
    - Diagnosis: Persistent high‑risk to “gru03” from 2024‑05‑06 to 2024‑07‑09 with high downstream RTT/jitter and depressed download throughput; recovery after 2024‑07‑09. Short July jitter‑driven degradations to “gru05” (RTT_download_std ~46 ms) and a modest event to “rnp_sp”; “gru06” and “rnp_rj” remain stable.
  - client02 (coef −0.652): High‑risk association.
    - Diagnosis: A single instability window 2024‑05‑06/08 to 2024‑06‑15 affecting “rnp_sp” (brief) and especially “rnp_rj” (four intervals, including 2024‑06‑02 → 2024‑06‑04 with lower DL throughput and high UL RTT variability). Outside this window, all targets are stable (including “gru03/05/06”).

- Most critical servers
  - rnp_rj (coef −0.903): Associated with high‑risk.
    - Diagnosis: 33/44 intervals stable; 11/44 high‑risk, concentrated in client01 (mid‑May → early June and mid‑July, with very high downstream RTT jitter) and client02 (May → mid‑June, including a throughput dip 2024‑06‑02 → 2024‑06‑04). All other clients to rnp_rj are stable, indicating client/path specificity rather than a systemic server issue.

- Metrics with greatest impact on stability (logistic regression on Cluster 1 vs Cluster 0)
  - rtt_download_std (−1.994): Higher downstream RTT jitter strongly increases odds of Cluster 0 (instability). This is the most powerful discriminator and aligns with the degraded intervals observed for client12 (gru05/gru03 spikes) and client01→rnp_rj.
  - throughput_download_mean (+1.320): Higher download throughput favors Cluster 1; lower values push toward Cluster 0. Key in client09’s persistent low DL throughput to “gru03” and client02’s dip to rnp_rj on 2024‑06‑02 → 2024‑06‑04.
  - throughput_upload_std (+0.828): Higher UL throughput variability is associated with Cluster 1 in this dataset; it is not a driver of instability here.
  - rtt_download_mean (−0.662): Higher downstream RTT increases odds of Cluster 0. Prominent in client09/client12 toward “gru03” before 2024‑07‑09 and in client01→rnp_rj mid‑May to early June.

## 3) Recommended Actions

- Client09 ↔ “gru03”
  - Run concurrent iperf3/NDT download tests from client09 (and at least one control client) to “gru03” vs “gru05/06”; capture server NIC/CPU/queue stats on “gru03”.
  - Execute traceroute/Paris‑traceroute/MTR to “gru03” to confirm post‑2024‑07‑09 path and rule out asymmetry.
  - If low DL throughput persists under low RTT and clean path, investigate server‑side limits or downstream shaping (policing, NIC offload, queue configuration).

- Client12 ↔ “gru03”
  - Perform time‑aligned traceroute/Paris‑traceroute and MTR to “gru03” (controls: “gru06”, “rnp_rj”) to localize hops with high downstream jitter/RTT pre‑2024‑07‑09.
  - Pull queue/ECN/drop counters on interfaces serving client12→gru03 for May–early July; correlate with change logs/BGP updates around 2024‑07‑07 to 2024‑07‑09.
  - Investigate the short July jitter spike possibly affecting a shared São Paulo segment (compare simultaneous paths to “gru03/05/rnp_sp”).

- Client02 ↔ “rnp_rj” (and brief “rnp_sp”)
  - Run traceroute/MTR during peak/off‑peak to detect path changes or per‑hop variance across 2024‑05‑06 to 2024‑06‑15.
  - Schedule controlled iperf3 tests with TCP diagnostics (retransmissions, cwnd) toward rnp_rj to confirm congestion signatures during the 2024‑06‑02 → 2024‑06‑04 dip.
  - Cross‑check RNP RJ/SP change logs over 2024‑05‑06–06‑15; prioritize RNP‑facing segment (peering/transport to RJ/SP) since “gru03/05/06” were stable.

- rnp_rj focused checks
  - Conduct simultaneous traceroute/MTR from client01 and rnp_rj toward client01, and from client02 to rnp_rj, targeting prior incident windows; correlate with BGP/IGP change logs (around 2024‑05‑07 and 2024‑07‑11).
  - Validate client01 access/aggregation queue behavior under load (bufferbloat checks).
  - Verify capacity headroom and policy/shaping changes around 2024‑06‑02 → 2024‑06‑04.

- Monitoring and alerting
  - Prioritize alerting on rtt_download_std excursions and sustained declines in throughput_download_mean, as these most strongly signal transitions to high‑risk states.
  - Maintain per‑target baselines to rapidly detect path‑specific degradations (especially to “gru03” and “rnp_rj”).
