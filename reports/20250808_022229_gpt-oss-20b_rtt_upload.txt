## QoS Assessment Report: rtt_upload Approach  

### 1. Executive Summary  
The rtt_upload analysis reveals a stark contrast between two behavioral regimes. **Cluster 0** (high‑risk) is characterized by short, highly‑volatile intervals, low throughput, and elevated upload RTTs (~ 98 ms). **Cluster 1** (low‑risk) shows long, stable periods with high throughput (~ 660 Mbps) and low upload RTTs (~ 20 ms). Five client/server pairs exhibit strong statistical association with the high‑risk cluster: `client09`, `client12`, `client02`, `client07`, and the backbone node `rnp_rj`. Among them, `client12` and `client07` display intermittent high‑risk episodes linked to specific server paths, while `client02` remains largely stable but occasionally experiences elevated RTT variability on RNP backbone links. `rnp_rj` remains consistently low‑risk, suggesting that the observed instabilities are primarily client‑side or path‑specific rather than destination‑driven.

### 2. Detailed Findings  

#### 2.1 Cluster Characteristics  
- **Cluster 0 (High‑Risk)**  
  - **Duration & Frequency**: Mean interval 10.5 days, event frequency 0.645.  
  - **Throughput**: Avg. download 307 Mbps, upload 281 Mbps.  
  - **Upload RTT**: Mean 98 ms, std. dev. high, indicating congestion or routing instability.  
  - **Survival**: 0.59 after 7 days, 0.11 after 60 days.  

- **Cluster 1 (Low‑Risk)**  
  - **Duration & Frequency**: Mean interval 31.5 days, event frequency 0.030.  
  - **Throughput**: Avg. download 661 Mbps, upload 660 Mbps.  
  - **Upload RTT**: Mean 20 ms, low variability.  
  - **Survival**: 1.0 up to 15 days, 0.961 at 30 days.  

#### 2.2 Critical Elements  
Logistic regression coefficients (log‑odds of belonging to Cluster 1) are strongly negative for the following entities, indicating a propensity toward the high‑risk cluster:

| Element | Coefficient |
|---------|-------------|
| client09 | -0.992 |
| rnp_rj   | -0.903 |
| client12 | -0.782 |
| client02 | -0.652 |
| client07 | -0.604 |

These values highlight the most problematic client/server pairs for upload RTT performance.

#### 2.3 Individual Diagnoses  

- **client12**  
  - **Temporal Pattern**: 56 % of intervals in Cluster 1, 44 % in Cluster 0.  
  - **Key Event**: Late‑June shift to Cluster 1 across all servers, brief return to Cluster 0 on `rnp_sp` (17–23 Jul).  
  - **Implication**: Path‑specific congestion or routing change on `rnp_sp`.  

- **client07**  
  - **Temporal Pattern**: 62 % Cluster 1, 38 % Cluster 0.  
  - **Key Event**: Persistent high‑risk on `gru05`, `gru06`, `rnp_rj`, `rnp_sp`; low‑risk only on `gru03`.  
  - **Implication**: Possible peering bottleneck or sub‑optimal routing on the majority of paths.  

- **client02**  
  - **Temporal Pattern**: ~90 % Cluster 1, ~10 % Cluster 0.  
  - **Key Event**: Mid‑June transition to Cluster 0 on `rnp_rj` and `rnp_sp` with increased RTT variability.  
  - **Implication**: Routing change or link degradation on RNP backbone.  

- **rnp_rj**  
  - **Temporal Pattern**: 100 % Cluster 0 (low‑risk).  
  - **Implication**: Stable destination; observed instabilities are not due to the server itself.  

- **client09**  
  - **Status**: Identified as high‑risk by coefficient but lacking interval‑level diagnostics in the provided data.  

### 3. Recommended Actions  

- **Path‑Specific Monitoring**  
  - Deploy continuous traceroute and pathping from `client12` to `rnp_sp` during the 17–23 Jul window to confirm routing changes.  
  - Run similar tests from `client07` to `gru05`, `gru06`, `rnp_rj`, and `rnp_sp` to detect asymmetric or congested links.  

- **BGP and Peering Review**  
  - Examine BGP update logs for `client02` during mid‑June to identify any prefix withdrawals or route advertisements that could explain the RTT spike on `rnp_rj`/`rnp_sp`.  
  - Coordinate with RNP peering partners to assess potential bottlenecks affecting the identified high‑risk paths.  

- **Client‑Side Load Analysis**  
  - Investigate local network utilization on `client12`, `client07`, and `client02` during high‑risk intervals to rule out client‑side congestion.  

- **Threshold Definition and Alerting**  
  - Set automated alerts for upload RTT > 25 ms or throughput < 700 Mbps on the identified paths, triggering traceroute and BGP checks.  

- **Documentation and Follow‑Up**  
  - Record all findings, including any observed routing changes, and schedule a follow‑up review after implementing corrective actions to verify stability improvements.  

These steps will isolate the root causes of upload RTT instability and enable targeted remediation, ensuring sustained QoS for the RNP network.
