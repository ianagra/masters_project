## QoS Assessment Report: throughput_download Approach

### 1) Executive Summary
Two distinct performance clusters emerged. Cluster 1 represents a low-risk, stable profile (longer intervals, low event frequency, higher download/upload throughput, and low RTT). Cluster 0 represents a high-risk, unstable profile (shorter intervals, high event frequency, lower throughput, and elevated RTT). Logistic regression associates client02, client07, rnp_rj, and rnp_sp with higher odds of Cluster 0. Time-series diagnoses show: (a) client07 shifted to unstable download performance from mid-June toward rnp_rj and rnp_sp without RTT inflation, and had earlier high-RTT issues to gru03 that later normalized; (b) client02 is predominantly Cluster 0, with notable download variability toward gru05; (c) rnp_rj and rnp_sp show coordinated mid-July download collapses across multiple clients while uploads and RTT largely remained stable, consistent with downstream capacity/policy effects near the servers or adjacent peering. Cluster labels align with aggregated risk, but are not a perfect proxy per client/site, so interval metrics and timing remain essential.

### 2) Detailed Findings

- Cluster risk profiles (aggregated)
  - Cluster 1 (low-risk, stable): Long intervals (≈24.29 days), very low event frequency (0.078), strong survival (≥0.887 at 30 days), higher throughput (DL ≈632.5, UL ≈684.3), and low RTT (DL ≈16.0 ms, UL ≈18.9 ms).
  - Cluster 0 (high-risk, unstable): Short intervals (≈10.64 days), high event frequency (0.738), fast decay (survival 0.553 at 7 days; ~0.18 at 15–30 days), lower throughput (DL ≈452.1, UL ≈429.3), and elevated RTT (DL ≈59.6 ms, UL ≈60.6 ms).

- Critical elements (logistic regression; higher odds of Cluster 0)
  - Clients: client02 (coef −1.95), client07 (coef −1.73).
  - Servers: rnp_rj (coef −1.37), rnp_sp (coef −1.23).

- Interval diagnoses

  client02
  - Cluster distribution: 24/28 intervals (86%) in Cluster 0; 4/28 (14%) in Cluster 1.
  - RNP paths (rnp_sp, rnp_rj): Mostly steady behavior across May–Jul with two short degradations:
    - rnp_rj: 2024-06-02 to 2024-06-04, DL dipped to ~554 Mbps with high variability (std ~78 Mbps); RTT_upload_mean ~25.38 ms.
    - rnp_sp: 2024-06-06 to 2024-06-07, DL ~579.7 Mbps with elevated variability (std ~37 Mbps); recovered afterward.
  - GRU paths (gru03, gru05, gru06): Present throughout; gru05 shows a long sub-interval 2024-05-11 to 2024-07-15 with lower DL (~557 Mbps) and very high variability (std ~92.6 Mbps), indicating sustained instability on that path.
  - Note: Although Cluster 1 is generally stable in aggregate, for client02 the GRU-assigned intervals include the most pronounced download variability (gru05), underscoring that cluster labels are not a perfect per-path proxy.

  client07
  - Cluster distribution: 17/27 (≈63%) in Cluster 0; 10/27 (≈37%) in Cluster 1.
  - Mid-June shift to instability toward RNP servers:
    - rnp_sp: from 2024-06-12, multiple change-point intervals move to Cluster 0; by 2024-07-06–07-23 DL mean ≈522 Mbps with extremely high variance (std ≈421 Mbps); RTT stable (~13–15 ms).
    - rnp_rj: from 2024-06-16, Cluster 0 intervals accumulate; 2024-07-06–07-22 DL ≈376–380 Mbps with very high std (≈304–405 Mbps); RTT ~18–20 ms and stable.
  - gru03 path: 2024-05-02 to 2024-07-09 had abnormally high RTT (~120–212 ms) with modest DL; 2024-07-09–07-17 transient high DL/low RTT but high variance; after 2024-07-17, stabilized with low RTT (~14–20 ms).
  - Consistently healthy to GRU: gru05 and gru06 stayed in Cluster 1 with high throughput and low RTT.

  rnp_rj
  - Cluster mix: 42/76 (≈55%) in Cluster 1; 34/76 (≈45%) in Cluster 0.
  - Coordinated mid-July download degradations (uploads and RTT roughly steady at ~18–24 ms):
    - 2024-07-17→07-23 affecting multiple clients: client08 (DL ~182.6 Mbps), client09 (~219.2 Mbps), client13 (~248.6 Mbps), client14 (~220.4 Mbps), client16 (~182.3 Mbps), and client07 (longer low-DL spell ~376–380 Mbps, 07-06→07-22).
  - Notable routing/latency change: client04 from 2024-06-26 shows RTT_upload ≈30.4 ms (up from ~2–3 ms) while maintaining high throughput—consistent with a path change rather than capacity loss.

  rnp_sp
  - Cluster mix: 34/58 (≈58.6%) in Cluster 1; 24/58 (≈41.4%) in Cluster 0.
  - Mid-July download ceiling across multiple clients with stable upload:
    - 2024-07-17→07-23: client08 (DL ~175.7 Mbps; RTT_download ~26.4 ms with high jitter), client12 (~232.6→~219.0 Mbps), client13 (~174→~177 Mbps), client14 (~176.5 Mbps), client16 (~176.3 Mbps). Upload sustained high (~790–830 Mbps); RTT mostly near historical values except client08.
  - Precursors:
    - client07: 2024-07-06→07-23, DL mean ≈522 Mbps with very high variance (std ≈421 Mbps), RTT stable.
    - client09: 2024-07-04→07-09, DL instability (mean ≈629 Mbps, std ≈387 Mbps); partial recovery with lingering variance.
  - Cluster label caveat: Some Cluster 0 intervals reflect high performance (e.g., client04 ~910.6 Mbps DL on 2024-06-26→07-01), so label ≠ universal “bad.”

### 3) Recommended Actions

- Correlate and reproduce mid-July degradations
  - Run parallel NDT/iperf tests during comparable hours from affected clients (07, 08, 09, 12, 13, 14, 16) to rnp_rj and rnp_sp, plus an unaffected control path (e.g., client02→RNP).
  - Capture TCP retransmissions/loss, cwnd evolution, and per-flow goodput; compare download vs upload asymmetries.

- Path and routing verification
  - Perform bidirectional traceroute/MTR during degradation windows; record AS paths/hop stability and per-hop loss/latency.
  - Review BGP/peering change logs and link utilizations around 2024-07-17→07-23 (rnp_rj and rnp_sp) and 2024-06-12→06-16 (client07 onset).

- Server-side and adjacent interface checks (rnp_rj, rnp_sp)
  - Inspect egress/ingress interface counters (drops, discards), queue/AQM stats, shaping/policing policies, and any recent config changes.
  - Specifically validate absence of download-path rate limits near ~200 Mbps on/near rnp_sp in 2024-07-17→07-23.

- Client-specific investigations
  - client07: Audit access/aggregation device counters for drops/oversubscription; verify no new policers/shapers introduced mid-June. Repeat sustained tests to rnp_rj/rnp_sp and confirm stable RTT with signs of loss-based throughput collapse.
  - client02: Target gru05/gru03/gru06 vs rnp_sp as control; correlate high download variability (2024-05-11→07-15 to gru05) with path/routing/interface data; check for intermittent congestion on GRU segments.

- Historic anomalies
  - Validate routing changes for client04→rnp_rj around 2024-06-26 that raised RTT without throughput loss.
  - For client07→gru03, confirm route normalization post 2024-07-09 with baseline RTT monitoring.

- Monitoring and alerting
  - Implement detectors for synchronized multi-client download depressions and for “download ceiling” patterns (e.g., ~200 Mbps) with unaffected upload/RTT.
  - Track cluster event rates per client/server to catch rising instability before customer impact.
